Thermodynamics is a theory built entirely from a macroscopic point of view. Still, if you look at what its "setups" and "operations" do at the microscopic level, the differences between work, heat, and internal energy become much easier to grasp. Here you move the particles in a container (micro) and check how temperature T, pressure P, volume V, internal energy U, work W, and heat Q (macro) are determined and how they change.

The model used in these simulations

  • A two-dimensional hard-disk gas (particles collide elastically). Dimensionless units with particle mass m = 1 and Boltzmann constant kB = 1.
  • Two-dimensional and monatomic, so there are 2 degrees of freedom: U = NkT, PV = NkT (V is area, P is force per unit length), heat capacity ratio γ = 2. The difference from the three-dimensional U = (3/2)NkT is the number of degrees of freedom.
  • Work W: the total change in kinetic energy when particles hit a moving piston or a rotating stirrer. Heat Q: the total change in kinetic energy when particles hit a diathermal wall in contact with a heat reservoir. Both are counted collision by collision, so ΔU = Q + W holds exactly (apart from rounding errors).
  • Adiabatic walls reflect particles like a mirror. A particle that hits a diathermal wall in contact with a reservoir bounces back with a velocity drawn from the Maxwell distribution at the reservoir temperature.
  • The sign convention is the same as in the lecture: W is the work done on the system, Q is the heat received by the system.
