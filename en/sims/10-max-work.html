Goal

Double the volume of an ideal gas at temperature T. In A, the piston is pulled slowly while the gas stays in contact with a reservoir (reversible isothermal expansion). In B, the partition of an adiabatic container is removed and the gas spreads into vacuum (free expansion). Both end in the same equilibrium state, "temperature T, twice the volume", with ΔU = 0.

Still, the heat Q and work W differ with the path (they are not state functions). The entropy change of the gas, on the other hand, is the same. The difference shows up in the entropy of the surroundings (the reservoir): the total entropy change is 0 for A and Nk ln 2 for B. The second law ΔS_total ≥ 0 puts an upper limit on the work that can be extracted, W_out ≤ −ΔF (the principle of maximum work).

Try it

  1. Press "Start the expansion". B spreads at once, while the piston in A moves slowly (you may raise the playback speed). Compare Q, W, and ΔU for A and B in the table.
  2. When it is over, compare "ΔS of the gas" with "ΔS of the reservoir". In A, the reservoir's S decreases by the heat it gave, and the total is almost 0. In B there is no exchange with a reservoir, and the total increases by N ln 2.
  3. The bar chart below: A extracts work all the way up to the limit −ΔF, B extracts 0. In B, all the work NkT ln 2 that could have been extracted is "lost".
  4. Choose "Fast" and try again. The work extracted in A decreases, and the total entropy increases by the corresponding amount (work lost TΔS_total). The sum always stays near −ΔF.

Theoretical values (2D ideal gas, k = 1)

Reversible isothermal expansion: W = −∫P dV = −NkT ln(V₂/V₁) = −NkT ln 2, Q = −W. Gas ΔS = Nk ln 2, reservoir ΔS = −Q/T = −Nk ln 2, 0 in total.

Free expansion: Q = W = 0 and ΔU = 0, so the temperature does not change and the final state is the same as for the reversible isothermal expansion. Gas ΔS = Nk ln 2 (a state function); there is no change in any reservoir, so the total is Nk ln 2.

The change in the Helmholtz free energy F = U − TS is ΔF = −NkT ln 2 in both cases. In a process that exchanges heat with a reservoir at temperature T, Q ≤ TΔS (the second law), so together with the first law W ≥ ΔF, and the extracted work is W_out = −W ≤ −ΔF. Moreover W_out = −ΔF − TΔS_total: irreversibility (ΔS_total > 0) reduces the work that can be extracted.

About the measured values

The particles are disks of finite size, so the pressure is slightly higher than for an ideal gas, and |W| in A comes out 2–3% larger than the theoretical value. For this reason, even when A is run slowly, the total ΔS can deviate from 0 by a few units (small compared with N ln 2 ≈ 139). Because the number of particles is small, the gas temperature and the reservoir's ΔS fluctuate, but their fluctuations cancel, so the total ΔS is smooth.
