Goal

If two fluids at different temperatures are put in contact through a diathermal wall and left alone, they eventually settle into a state with no macroscopic change (an equilibrium state). We look at the lecture's "coffee and room" example microscopically.

Try it

  1. Wait with the adiabatic wall in place. Do T₁ and T₂ change?
  2. Switch to "Diathermal wall". Which way does the energy move? How does the particle speed change at the wall? ("Color = speed" makes this easy to see.)
  3. Wait long enough. What happens to T₁ and T₂? Do U₁ and U₂ become equal? (Try it with N₁ ≠ N₂.)
  4. Even after reaching equilibrium, the particles keep moving. What are the small fluctuations in the graphs?
  5. Finally, "Remove" the partition. The two fluids mix. Can you wait until they return to the original state?
Question In equilibrium T₁ = T₂, but not necessarily U₁ = U₂. Which kind of variable becomes equal in equilibrium: "intensive variables" or "extensive variables"? (→ check this in 05)

About the model

The diathermal wall is modeled as "a wall with a small heat capacity". When a particle hits the wall, it bounces back with a velocity drawn from the Maxwell distribution at the wall temperature Tw, and the wall's energy changes by the same amount. Particles on both sides exchange energy with the same wall, so heat flows between the left and right fluids through the wall (the total energy of gas + wall is conserved exactly).
