Goal

Lift a parcel of air a little: the surrounding pressure drops, so it expands adiabatically and cools (the same adiabatic expansion as in 03). It cools at the dry adiabatic lapse rate Γd = g/cp ≈ 9.8 K/km. If the surrounding temperature falls more slowly than that, the lifted parcel is colder and heavier than its surroundings and is pushed back (stable). If the surroundings fall faster, the parcel is warmer and lighter and keeps rising (unstable → convection).

The parcel moves adiabatically, so it keeps its potential temperature θ (that is, its entropy). An atmosphere whose potential temperature (entropy) increases upward is stable; if it decreases upward, it is unstable. Comparing entropy rather than temperature tells you which way the parcel moves.

Try it

  1. With Γenv = 6.5 K/km (an average troposphere), press "Lift and release". The parcel oscillates up and down. What is the period?
  2. Make Γenv larger than 9.8 K/km. What happens to the lifted parcel? In the potential-temperature plot, θ now decreases upward.
  3. Make Γenv negative (warmer aloft: an inversion). Is the oscillation faster? Why do smoke and fog stay near the ground on winter mornings?
  4. What about Γenv = Γd exactly (uniform θ)?

Summary of formulas (dry air)

From the first law per unit mass and hydrostatic balance dP/dz = −ρg, a parcel moving adiabatically while keeping the surrounding pressure satisfies cp dT = v dP, i.e. dT/dz = −g/cp = −Γd (approximating a small density difference from the surroundings).

Displace a parcel by ξ from a height z₀ where it has the surrounding temperature: the temperature difference is (Γenv − Γd)ξ and the buoyant acceleration is g(Tparcel − Tenv)/Tenv. Together, d²ξ/dt² = −N²ξ with N² = (g/T)(Γd − Γenv). If N² > 0 the parcel oscillates with period 2π/N (minutes to ten-odd minutes); if N² < 0 it moves away exponentially. This page ignores friction and mixing with the surroundings.

The height change of potential temperature is (1/θ)dθ/dz = (Γd − Γenv)/T, the same sign as N². The entropy of dry air is s = cp ln θ + const.

The surrounding pressure comes from hydrostatic balance and the equation of state. Isothermal: P = Ps e−gz/(RdT) (the same Boltzmann factor as sedimentation of molecules); constant lapse rate Γ: P = Ps(T/Ts)g/(RdΓ).
