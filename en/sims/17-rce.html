Conditions (may be changed while running)

Increasing τs corresponds to adding infrared-absorbing gases (water vapor, CO₂) in the gray approximation. Γcrit = 9.8 is the dry adiabatic lapse rate; 6.5 is a typical value for convection including condensation of water vapor.

Goal

Write the first law "heat capacity × rate of temperature change = inflow − outflow" not for the whole Earth but for each thin layer of the atmosphere. Updating temperatures little by little from each layer's radiative inflow and outflow, radiation alone makes the lapse rate near the surface too large, and the atmosphere becomes convectively unstable (14). In reality convection carries heat upward and keeps the lapse rate near a critical value. Representing this by energy-conserving convective adjustment gives radiative-convective equilibrium, where radiative cooling and convective heating balance.

Although called an equilibrium, it is a steady state, not a thermodynamic equilibrium: energy from the Sun keeps entering at the surface, being carried upward by convection and radiation, and leaving to space.

Try it

  1. Play and wait until both profiles settle into a steady state. How many K warmer is the radiation-only surface than the one with convection? In both, Jabs = OLR at the top of the atmosphere.
  2. Heating-rate plot: in the troposphere, radiation cools (negative) and convection warms (positive). In the stratosphere (above where convection reaches) both are 0.
  3. Increase τs (strengthen the greenhouse effect). The surface warms; what happens aloft? The tropopause height?
  4. With Γcrit = 9.8 (dry), is the surface temperature higher or lower than with 6.5?

Inside the model (an educational gray atmosphere)

The upward and downward longwave fluxes at the layer boundaries are computed from below and from above (the formulas of section 8.1): Ui = tiUi−1 + (1 − ti)σTi⁴, Di−1 = tiDi + (1 − ti)σTi⁴, with U₀ = σTs⁴ at the surface and D = 0 from space. Layer transmission is t = exp(−1.66Δτ), with optical depth concentrated in the lower layers, τ(P) = τs(P/Ps)².

Each layer's temperature is advanced in 6-hour steps with (cpΔP/g) dT/dt = inflow − outflow (eq. 8.2). All solar radiation is absorbed at the surface (for simplicity). In the column with convection, after each update, any part where the lapse rate of the surface and layers exceeds Γcrit is set onto a Γcrit line while keeping the heat-capacity-weighted total energy. Layer heights come from hydrostatic balance, dz = −(RdT/g) d ln P.

This is an educational model to show the idea of radiative-convective equilibrium; it does not reproduce the 1967 Manabe–Wetherald model (wavelength-dependent absorption, water vapor set by fixed relative humidity, etc.). Being gray, details such as the temperature aloft do not match reality.
