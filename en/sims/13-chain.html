Goal

A chain of Ns segments of length b, each pointing in a random direction (an ideal chain). Every shape has the same energy, yet a force is needed to hold the ends apart. The reason is the number of configurations: there are many shapes with the end near the origin, but few with the end far away. Stretching lowers the entropy, so in surroundings at temperature T the free energy F = U − TS rises. Its slope is the force f = dF/dx.

Let go and the chain contracts, because the number of configurations (entropy) increases in that direction ― once again, the second law decides the direction of change.

Try it

  1. Play and make many chains. The ends (gray dots) gather around the origin and become rarer farther away. The distribution in the upper plot approaches a Gaussian.
  2. Change the "Extension". How much smaller is the fraction P(x) of ends in the band compared with x = 0? That is the decrease in configurational entropy, ΔS = k ln(P(x)/P(0)).
  3. In the lower plot F(x) is a parabola. Its slope (the tangent) is the force needed, proportional to x (Hooke's law). Same form as a spring, but the origin is entropy, not energy.
  4. What happens to the force when you double the temperature? (The distribution does not change, but the force is proportional to T.)
  5. Increase the number of segments Ns. What is the force at the same x? (Longer chains are softer.)
Question Hang a weight on a rubber band and warm it with a hair dryer. What happens? Why is it the opposite of an ordinary metal spring? (f ∝ T)
Question Stretch a rubber band quickly and touch it to your lips: it feels warm. When it is stretched adiabatically, what makes up for the decrease in configurational entropy? (The total entropy does not decrease.)
Question When you stretch slowly, where does the work W = ΔF go? Think from ΔU = 0 for an ideal chain and the first law.
Theory (2D ideal chain)

The x coordinate of the end is x = Σ b cos θj. The terms are independent with mean 0 and ⟨cos²θ⟩ = 1/2, so σ² = ⟨x²⟩ = Nsb²/2 (in 3D, Nsb²/3). As a sum of many independent contributions, the distribution is approximately Gaussian, P(x) ∝ e−x²/2σ² (central limit theorem).

The ratio of the numbers of configurations with the end in bands of equal width is P(x)/P(0), so S(x) − S(0) = k ln(P(x)/P(0)) = −kx²/2σ². For an ideal chain the energy does not depend on the shape, so F(x) − F(0) = −T(S(x) − S(0)) = kTx²/2σ². From the equality of minimum work δW = dF, the external force is f = dF/dx = kTx/σ² = 2kTx/(Nsb²) (in 3D, 3kTx/(Nsb²)).

The Gaussian approximation holds when the end-to-end distance is much shorter than the full chain length Nsb. In this material the length b = 1 and k = 1.
