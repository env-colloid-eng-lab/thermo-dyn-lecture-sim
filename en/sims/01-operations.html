Time evolution

Micro↔MacroSpeed distribution of the particles

Temperature T is the mean kinetic energy per particle (in 2D, U = NkT). If the shape of the distribution matches the Maxwell distribution at the same T, the gas is in an "equilibrium state". The gray dashed line is the initial (T=1) distribution.
Kinetic energy is proportional to the square of the speed, so even if the temperature doubles, the speed only grows by a factor of √2 ≈ 1.4 (⟨v⟩ = √(πT/2)). If the difference is hard to see, compare using the colors, the arrows, and this distribution.

Goal

Check how the walls and Operations 1-3 from the "setup" in the lecture correspond to particle motion (micro) and the state variables T, P, V, U (macro).

Try it

  1. With all walls adiabatic, move the piston to the left (compression). Particles that hit the piston bounce back faster. What happens to T and U? And W?
  2. Move the piston to the right (expansion). Now the particles bounce back slower. The gas "did" work on the outside (W < 0).
  3. Turn the stirrer. U increases even though the volume does not change (Operation 2 is also a mechanical operation). Does turning it the other way bring things back?
  4. Choose "Contact a reservoir". What is different when the left wall is adiabatic and when it is diathermal?
  5. Choose "Place in surroundings", make all walls diathermal, and wait. T settles to the temperature of the surroundings (transition to an equilibrium state).
Question Seen microscopically, both work W and heat Q are "energy exchanged in collisions between particles and walls". So what is the difference between them? (Hint: W comes from a wall that a person moves, Q from a wall in contact with a heat reservoir. Which one can a person control?)
About the model

A two-dimensional hard-disk gas (dimensionless units with mass m=1 and kB=1). Particles collide elastically with each other. Adiabatic walls reflect like a mirror; a particle that hits a diathermal wall in contact with a reservoir bounces back with a velocity drawn from the Maxwell distribution at the reservoir temperature. Being two-dimensional and monatomic, the gas has 2 degrees of freedom, so U = NkT and PV = NkT (V is area, P is force per unit length).

For visibility, particles are drawn larger than their actual collision radius.
