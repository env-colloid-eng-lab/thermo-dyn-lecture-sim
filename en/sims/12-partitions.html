Goal

Divide an isolated system (outer walls adiabatic and fixed) into two chambers with a partition. What exchanges the partition allows decides which quantities become equal in equilibrium. In every case the allowed changes proceed in the direction that increases the total entropy, and equilibrium is where no room for increase is left (the second law).

  • Diathermal, fixed (energy only) → T₁ = T₂
  • Diathermal, movable (energy and volume) → T₁ = T₂ and P₁ = P₂
  • Lets particles through (energy and particles) → T₁ = T₂ and μ₁ = μ₂

Try it

  1. Wait with "Adiabatic, fixed". None of the coefficients is 0, yet nothing changes. The partition forbids every exchange (a constraint).
  2. Switch to "Diathermal, fixed". Only the temperature coefficient heads to 0, and T₁ = T₂. Do the pressures become equal? (With N₁ ≠ N₂ the fixed wall supports the pressure difference.)
  3. Switch to "Diathermal, movable". The wall moves, and the pressures become equal as well as the temperatures. Where does the wall stop? (V₁/V₂ = N₁/N₂)
  4. Switch to "Lets particles through". Particles move back and forth, and the temperatures and number densities (chemical potentials) become equal.
  5. In every case the "total ΔS" increases and then levels off.
Question When equilibrium is reached with a fixed diathermal wall, may you conclude that P₁ = P₂? If the wall is allowed to move, which term of the formula newly remains?
Question Even if the concentrations differ between left and right, a wall that does not let particles through does not remove the difference. Is it right to say "if there is a concentration difference, particles always move"? When stating an equilibrium, specify what exchanges it allows.
Question (Extra) With an adiabatic movable wall, the pressures become equal quickly, but the temperatures take a long time to become equal. Why does energy move between left and right when the wall moves, even though the wall does not pass heat?
How the coefficients are obtained (2D ideal gas)

The entropy of each chamber is S = Nk[ln(V/N) + ln T] + N·const. In an isolated system dU₂ = −dU₁, dV₂ = −dV₁, dN₂ = −dN₁, so writing dS = dS₁ + dS₂ gives the formula above (∂S/∂U = 1/T, ∂S/∂V = P/T, ∂S/∂N = −μ/T).

Set the changes the partition forbids to 0 and set the coefficients of the allowed independent changes to 0 to get the equilibrium conditions. For a 2D ideal gas P/T = n and μ/T = ln(n/T) + const, so once the temperatures are equal, both the pressure condition and the particle condition become "equal number densities".

The total ΔS in the plot is the change from the start of the sum of the two gases and the diathermal wall (heat capacity Cw), N₁ ln(V₁T₁/N₁) + N₂ ln(V₂T₂/N₂) + Cw ln Tw.

About the model

The movable partition is a wall of mass M that exchanges momentum with the particles in collisions. If it is diathermal, it sends particles back with the Maxwell distribution at the wall temperature, in the frame moving with the wall. The sum of the gas energy, the wall's internal energy and the wall's kinetic energy is conserved exactly. The wall itself has thermal motion, so it keeps jiggling a little even in equilibrium.

The "lets particles through" wall lets every particle pass (the colors only mark which side each particle started on). It is the same kind of gas, so mixing produces no mixing entropy (the same as "do not distinguish" in 07).
