Goal

Changes that happen spontaneously in an adiabatic container (free expansion, mixing, heat flow) never happen spontaneously in reverse. The state variable that expresses this "one-way" character is entropy S. For an isolated system, ΔS ≥ 0 (the second law of thermodynamics).

Try it

  1. In "Free expansion", remove the partition. The particles spread to the right and do not come back to the left. ln W settles near the theoretical ΔS = N ln 2.
  2. Repeat with N = 4 or 10. You may catch a moment when all particles are back on the left. What about large N? (Look at the "Probability" readout.)
  3. Remove the partition and immediately press "Reverse velocities": the particles head back to the left. What if you wait a while before reversing? (Also read "Why doesn't reversing the velocities bring it back?" below.)
  4. "Mixing": A and B mix and do not separate again. With "Do not distinguish" checked, "mixing" two samples of the same gas gives ΔS ≈ 0.
  5. "Heat flow": bring two gases at different temperatures into contact through a diathermal wall. Heat flows from the hot side to the cold side, and the total S increases.
Question Collisions between particles and reflections at the walls both obey laws of mechanics that hold equally well with time reversed. Why, then, do we never see a change "undo itself" spontaneously? (Use the number of microstates to think about how special a state is in which every particle's velocity is exactly reversed.)
Question In "Heat flow", the heat Q₂ received by the cold side and the heat −Q₁ lost by the hot side have almost the same magnitude. Why, then, is the total entropy change Q₂/T₂ + Q₁/T₁ positive?
Counting entropy (Boltzmann's formula)

Divide the container into left and right halves. If the number of ways to assign particles that corresponds to the macrostate "n particles on the left" is W = C(N, n) = N!/(n!(N−n)!), then S = k ln W.

At first all particles are on the left (W = 1, S = 0). The number of microstates is largest at n ≈ N/2, where ln C(N, N/2) ≈ N ln 2. This agrees with the entropy change of free expansion obtained from thermodynamics, ΔS = Nk ln(V₂/V₁) = Nk ln 2 (the agreement improves as N grows).

The probability that all particles return to the left is (1/2)N. For N = 200 it is about 10−60: it would not happen even if you waited for the age of the universe.

Why doesn't reversing the velocities bring it back?

With ideal (error-free) elastic-collision mechanics, if you reverse every particle's velocity exactly, the particles retrace their paths and always return to the start, no matter how long you waited. Mechanics itself holds with time reversed, so waiting by itself does not destroy reversibility.

In this simulation, reversing after a wait fails because of the error from computing in small time steps and from numerical rounding. These tiny errors grow with every collision (chaos) until the motion is completely off track. In a real gas, too, no one can reverse every particle's velocity perfectly, and any slight mismatch is quickly amplified. "Going back to the start" is, in terms of the number of microstates, a very special and practically impossible state.

Entropy of heat flow (2D ideal gas)

At constant volume dS = dQ/T and CV = Nk, so S = Nk ln T + const. The display shows the sum over the left gas, the right gas and the partition (heat capacity Cw): S = N₁ ln T₁ + N₂ ln T₂ + Cw ln Tw. Because the number of particles is finite, the temperatures fluctuate, and so S fluctuates slightly too.
