Goal

Above a water surface, molecules go back and forth between liquid and vapor. Moving dn from liquid to vapor changes the Gibbs free energy by dG = (μvapor − μliquid)dn. At constant temperature and pressure the change proceeds in the direction that lowers G (= the second law), so water moves from the phase with higher chemical potential to the one with lower. The vapor pressure at which the two are equal is the saturation vapor pressure es(T).

Microscopically, the number of molecules leaving the surface depends only on temperature, and the number entering is proportional to the amount of vapor. Where the two balance is saturation: no net transfer (molecules keep going back and forth).

Try it

  1. Play with "Lid on". The vapor increases and e approaches es(T) and stops (relative humidity 100%).
  2. Raise the temperature. es increases by about 6–7% per 1 K. More evaporates until the new saturation.
  3. After saturation, lower the temperature. The dot goes above the curve (supersaturated), vapor condenses and e drops. The temperature where condensation starts is the dew point.
  4. What happens when "Add vapor" makes it supersaturated? And "Remove half"? In both cases the sign of Δμ decides which way the water moves.
  5. With "Blown away by wind", saturation is never reached and evaporation continues (laundry dries).

Summary of formulas

Phase equilibrium: μliquid(T, p) = μvapor(T, p). Requiring coexistence as the temperature changes gives the Clapeyron equation dp/dT = Δs/Δv = L/(TΔv). Neglecting the liquid volume and treating the vapor as an ideal gas gives d ln es/dT = Lv/(RvT²) (Clausius–Clapeyron). Integrating with constant latent heat, es(T) = es(T₀) exp[(Lv/Rv)(1/T₀ − 1/T)]. Here 6.11 hPa at T₀ = 273.15 K, Lv = 2.5×10⁶ J/kg, Rv = 461 J/(kg K).

For an ideal-gas vapor μvapor(T, e) = μvapor(T, es) + RT ln(e/es), and at saturation μvapor(T, es) = μliquid, so Δμ = RT ln(e/es) (per mol).

In the stochastic model above, the rate of leaving the surface is set to match es(T); intermolecular attraction is not computed (the hard disks in this material have no attraction, so they cannot form a liquid). The rate of entering is proportional to the rate at which vapor molecules hit the surface.
