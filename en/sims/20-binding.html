Goal

Binding of a ligand L to a receptor R, R + L ⇄ RL, is like the two-state model of 11: a competition between "lower energy (bound)" and "moving freely over a large area (unbound)". In equilibrium the fraction bound is f = c/(Kd + c) (textbook section 4.8, equation (4.13)). c is the concentration of free ligand and Kd is the dissociation constant: the concentration at which exactly half the receptors are occupied.

Each receptor holds at most one ligand, so however high the concentration, f never exceeds 1: it saturates.

Try it

  1. Play and watch the fraction bound f settle around the theory (dashed). Binding and unbinding both continue, but the numbers binding and leaving balance.
  2. Change the number of ligands, press "Start over", and when it settles press "Record present values". Do several points lie on the curve f = c/(Kd + c)? Is f = 1/2 at c = Kd?
  3. Use very many ligands. f approaches 1 but does not exceed it (saturation).
  4. A larger ε (stronger binding) makes Kd smaller, so binding is strong even at low concentration. By what factor does Kd change when ε is lowered by 1?
  5. Raise the temperature T and bound ligands leave. Moving freely (larger entropy) wins over the energy gained by binding.

Deriving the equilibrium formula

At constant temperature and area, the chemical potential of a free ligand is μL = kT ln(c λ²) (λ is a length set by the temperature). A ligand caught by a receptor has energy lower by ε within an area πa², so per site equilibrium is f/(1 − f) = c · πa² · eε/kT (the free energy change of binding = 0). Rewriting gives f = c/(Kd + c), Kd = e−ε/kT/(πa²).

Per mole, Kd = c° eΔrG°/RT (the textbook formula). In ΔrG° = ΔrH° − TΔrS°, −ε is the energy (enthalpy) part and πa² is the entropy part of "being confined in a small space".

Amount added vs. free amount (conservation of the total)

If NL ligands and M receptors are added and n of them are bound, the free ligand concentration is c = (NL − n)/A (A: area of the container). Combined with the equilibrium condition n/(M − n) = c/Kd, this gives a quadratic equation for n. The "theory from the totals" below is its solution. Binding reduces the free ligand, so computing with the amount added overestimates binding.

About the model

2D hard-disk gas (m = 1, k = 1). Receptors are fixed points; a ligand binds when its center comes within radius a = 1 (at most one per site). A bound ligand stays at the site and leaves at the rate koff = kon e−ε/kT/(πa²) (kon = a√(2πkT/m) is the binding rate from kinetic theory), flying off with a speed drawn from the temperature of the surroundings. With this combination of rates the equilibrium fraction agrees with the thermodynamic formula. The ledger adds up every single energy change, so ΔU = Q + W holds exactly.
